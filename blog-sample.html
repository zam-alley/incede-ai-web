Why we ship a reference on day 1

Every Orchestrate engagement we've run has hit the same pivot moment around day 4: the customer's security or platform team asks for a diagram. If you don't have one ready, you lose a week. If your diagram is generic, you lose two weeks while it gets reshaped to look like the customer's real stack.

So we ship a real diagram on day 1. Not a vendor slide. A working, reviewable architecture that names the systems the customer actually runs.

Identity & SSO

Identity is the boring part that most pilots get wrong. The agent should never carry its own credentials to a system of record. The user's identity flows through Orchestrate, into the tool call, and into the audit log — which means OIDC or SAML federation to the customer's IdP, plus role mapping that lines up with the agent's scope.

We use Okta, Entra ID, and IBM Verify the most. Whatever your IdP, we wire it in week 2.

Integration boundary

The right-hand column of the diagram changes by industry. In healthcare it's Epic, claims, and a PHI vault. In FSI it's a core banking system, complaint queues, and the data warehouse. In manufacturing it's SAP, MES, and supplier portals. The control plane and governance plane don't change — and that's the point: most of the durable architectural decisions live to the left of that boundary, where the customer has the least muscle memory.

Governance plane

watsonx.governance is the under-talked-about piece of the platform. It's what gives audit, internal risk, and compliance teams something tangible to review — and it's the difference between an agent that ships and an agent that lives in a pilot tenant for six months.

Observability

Every Orchestrate engagement we run wires traces and metrics into the customer's existing observability stack — Datadog, Splunk, IBM Instana, whatever they already have on-call. We don't ask teams to learn a new pane of glass for the agents.

What we deliberately leave out

You'll notice the diagram doesn't include a vector store as a first-class component. That's deliberate. In our experience, the durable governance battle isn't won at the embedding layer; it's won in the control plane. Vector stores show up later, behind a tool boundary, when there's a real retrieval need — not by default.

Same with model fine-tuning. The right time to talk about fine-tuning is after you've shipped agents on baseline models and you have evidence the model is the bottleneck. Most of the time it isn't.
